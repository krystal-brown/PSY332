# PSY332
Behavioral analysis and offender profiling projects from PSY 332 Criminal Minds, including my psychological profile of the Gardner Museum art thieves using the CRIME method.

## Course Overview
PSY 332 Criminal Minds explores how psychological theory is applied to criminal behavior, offender profiling, and investigative practice. Throughout this course, I developed skills in behavioral analysis, ethical reasoning, and evidence based interpretation. These skills directly support my long term goals in digital forensics, criminal justice, and investigative work.

This repository includes my major course artifact, reflections, and thematic connections that show how psychology informs real world investigative processes.

## Final Project Psychological Profile of the Gardner Museum Art Thieves
Artifact Project Two Psychological Profile Using the CRIME Method  
Purpose Apply behavioral science to a real world unsolved case  
Skills Demonstrated Profiling, evidence interpretation, critical thinking, ethical analysis

This project analyzes the Isabella Stewart Gardner Museum art thieves using the CRIME method to develop a psychological profile based on offender behavior, motivation, and evidence patterns. I evaluated offender characteristics, decision making, and behavioral indicators to determine whether the thieves were organized, experienced, and purposeful in their actions.

Completing this project strengthened my ability to:

• Analyze offender behavior through a structured psychological lens  
• Interpret evidence patterns and behavioral clues  
• Apply academic theory to real investigative scenarios  
• Communicate findings in a clear and professional format  

This artifact demonstrates interdisciplinary strengths across psychology, criminal justice, and digital forensics.

## Ethics in Profiling
Ethical behavior is essential in any investigative or psychological field. This course emphasized the importance of avoiding bias, protecting individuals rights, recognizing the limits of profiling, interpreting evidence responsibly, and maintaining objectivity.

In my Gardner Museum project, I reflected on the ethical responsibility to avoid over interpreting limited evidence and to acknowledge the boundaries of behavioral profiling. This ethical awareness is important in fields involving sensitive information or vulnerable individuals.
